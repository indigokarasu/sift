# Dye Transfer Stain Removal — Fabric-Specific Guide

Condensed research from May 2026 session on removing color bleed/dye transfer from clothes.

## The Core Problem

Dye transfer (color run) occurs when loose dyes from dark/colored fabrics migrate to lighter fabrics during washing. The transferred dye bonds to the fibers of the affected garment. Removal difficulty depends entirely on **fiber type**.

## Cotton / Natural Fibers

- **Rit Color Remover** (sodium hydrosulfite) — primary recommendation. Designed for natural fibers. Dissolves direct/reactive dyes.
- **OxiClean / oxygen bleach** (sodium percarbonate) — effective secondary option. Works via oxidation.
- **Chlorine bleach** — effective on white cotton but can damage fibers over time.
- Method: Hot water soak (as hot as care label allows), 30 min – overnight.

## Synthetic Fibers (Polyester, Nylon, Polyamide, Spandex)

**Critical: Rit Color Remover is NOT effective on synthetics.** Its active ingredient (sodium hydrosulfite) is designed for natural fiber dyes.

Synthetic fibers use **disperse dyes** that bond at the molecular level under heat. Transferred disperse dye onto synthetic fabric is extremely stubborn.

### Recommended approach for synthetics:

1. **Carbona Color Run Remover** — rated highly for color accidents, works on synthetics
2. **OxiClean White Revive** — partial effectiveness, worth trying for brightening
3. **Clear Ammonia soak** — 1/2 cup clear ammonia + 2 tbsp liquid detergent in hot water, soak overnight, then wash separately in hot water. Ammonia breaks down disperse dye.
4. **Zout Triple Enzyme** — enzyme-based, safe on synthetics

### Warnings for synthetics:
- **Do NOT use chlorine bleach on spandex/elastan** — causes permanent damage
- Heat helps but don't exceed care label maximum
- Multiple treatments likely needed
- The Creative Folk, Clorox, and houseandhomeonline sources all confirm: Rit Color Remover works best on natural fibers; chlorine bleach works better on synthetics but damages fabric
